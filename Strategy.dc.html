Design brief

The easiest student loan application we can build.

Few questions, prefilled wherever we can, and no surprises about credit checks. It has to feel easy to a student fresh out of high school and calm and respectful to a grandparent asked to cosign. The two phones on this canvas are working prototypes of the student and the cosigner journeys, rebuilt after the SoFi, College Ave and Earnest reviews. The drafted legal text is plugged in where each piece belongs, and this page holds the reasoning and the decisions still open.

What we kept, changed and dropped

Emre's reaction was to the pacing and the roadmap. That points to SoFi's rhythm and Earnest's up-front honesty, with College Ave's cosigner mechanics and disclosure timing underneath.

From SoFi

Kept. The first screen that asks for the person's information straight away, with the student or cosigner tap. The birthday and citizenship questions. The loan period with a Most popular tag. The consent pop-up from a link, the soft check line in bold, the plain explanation for the Social Security number, and the reminder emails.

Changed. The cosigner is offered, never pushed, and the answer to the income question decides how it is raised. Income is asked in words that are easy to say yes to. Housing is simpler: rent, own, or stay with friends or family for free.

Dropped. Credit score monitoring, the broad consent to future products, and any pressure pop-up.

From Earnest

Kept. Its honesty about where the soft check ends. A review page with an Edit option on each section. A dashboard with status and document upload, used later when we need more. The idea of putting all partner wording in one place. Explaining how the phone number is used.

Changed. A short first step for name, email and phone so we always have a lead, then prefill, instead of one long form. A text code instead of a password. No second round of questions after sign-in.

Dropped. Account creation with an email and password, the school search, degree and program questions, and the five business day wait.

From College Ave

Kept. The automatic loan amount calculation, with the requested amount capped at what is calculated and information buttons beside each line. The confirm step. The early placement of the disclosure. The yes or no cosigner choice that opens a box. The school form for every student.

Changed. Tuition and fees only, because we do not lend for books or living costs. Cosigner invite by text, with a way to get the steps and share them later. The e-sign wording no longer suggests the loan is already granted.

Dropped. Entry screens, the new or existing account question, and the long borrower form.

Twelve decisions that remove friction

Each one is visible in the prototype. Open the student phone and press Play.

1

The school sends them. The link carries the school and its cost estimate, so there is no school search and no program quiz.

2

No password. A text code signs them in and brings them back. A passkey can follow later.

3

Few screens, prefilled. Name, email and phone first, so we always have a lead. Then a code, then one page that confirms what we filled in. Eight screens to a rate, and the legal gates sit on the screens where they apply.

4

The right keyboard every time. Number pad for numbers, email keyboard for email, and browser autofill hints on every field.

5

Fields format themselves. Phone, birthday, SSN and dollars add their own punctuation. No format errors to fix.

6

One button, same place. Fixed at the bottom, in thumb reach, and it wakes up when the screen is complete.

7

Big targets and bigger text. Choices are full-width cards at least 60 pixels tall, and an Aa button at the top makes everything larger in one tap. Built for a grandparent as much as a student.

8

Help and legal text in sheets. A link slides up the full text and leaves the form exactly as it was. A plain summary always sits on the screen itself.

9

Autosave, visibly. A Saved mark and a text link to resume. Leaving never costs anyone their work.

10

SSN only if needed. Asked only when our identity check cannot match, next to a plain reason and a statement of what is a soft check and what is not.

11

Edit and return. Fixing a detail from the review screen brings them straight back to review.

12

Always know what is next. After submit, every step shows who is doing what, in order, with a place to add documents later if we ask. No five day waits.

The path

The order follows the drafted disclosure suite: the combined consent first, then the Application and Solicitation Disclosure on the same first page, then the application, then the Approval Disclosure, the school form, the loan agreement and the Final Disclosure. Target: about 3 minutes to a rate. The time on each screen is an estimate to calibrate in testing.

Eight screens to a rate

Start: student or cosigner, name, email, phone, one combined consent, and the rates and fees block. Text code. Is this you, with birthday, address and citizenship prefilled. Income, with the cosigner question. Amount, calculated for them. Review, with Edit on each line. Credit check, with the SSN only if needed. Bank connection.

Then the rate. If a cosigner is needed and was not already invited, the same slot offers one, as an option. If we cannot confirm something, it becomes a request for documents. No account step, and no second round of questions.

After the rate

Lock in, with its own hard inquiry authorization. Approval Disclosure and accept. School self-certification. Loan agreement and Privacy Notice. Status tracker, with the Final Disclosure and the 3 business day cancel window. Optional marketing opt-in last.

Not designed yet. Decline and expired-link screens, and the Final Disclosure itself.

The cosigner

A text arrives, written so nobody feels pushed. The first screen says in plain words what cosigning means, that they can say no, and that a person can walk them through it. One combined consent, then the Notice to Cosigner on its own screen. A page that confirms details we filled in, an income question that counts Social Security and pensions, and their credit check. Larger text is one tap away. The student is told right away.

Consents: never more than one decision per box

The drafted consent bundle says each consent has its own legal basis and must be its own control, with no master checkbox and none pre-checked. The answer is not a wall of boxes. It is the right box on the screen where the decision happens.

Equifax, Prism and requests for more documents

These came from the Equifax consumer schedule, the Prism Data addendum and the October 9 consent review. Paragraphs added for them carry a [NEW] tag in the legal text, so counsel can find them.

Equifax credit pulls

The credit authorization names Equifax Information Services LLC and states that it is the borrower's written instruction, which is one of the permissible purposes in the Equifax schedule. It lists the purposes: evaluating the application, verifying it, and later reviewing, servicing and collecting.

It says plainly that checking the rate is a soft inquiry and that locking in is a hard inquiry. It covers the adverse action notice, with Equifax's name and the 60 day free report, and reporting the loan to Equifax. Confirm Equifax's current address and number

Vermont requires consent specific to the purpose of each pull. The authorization is written purpose by purpose to fit that.

Prism and bank data

The bank connection consent names Prism Data Technologies, Inc. through Equifax, and says the bank transactions are used to produce a cash flow score that informs the decision and the rate.

The addendum lets Equifax and Prism use the data, in aggregated or de-identified form, to test and improve their services. The consent says so in plain words. Confirm against the executed addendum

The addendum also has us warrant that we hold every consent needed to send them the data. This screen is that consent.

Asking for more

The credit authorization now says that if we cannot confirm identity, income, employment or enrollment, we may ask for more information or documents, may contact the sources the borrower named, and may be unable to finish if they are not provided. The same point appears in plain words on the screen.

A third outcome on the prototype shows the request itself: ID, proof of income and bank statements, each added with a tap. A route for someone who cannot connect a bank is built in.

An assistant that helps people ask the right questions

It does not replace the screens. It sits beside them, explains in plain words, and shows what is worth asking next. In the prototype every answer is written by hand and cites the document it came from. In production it is a model that may only answer from approved text.

How it works

Guide, a named assistant with a face, sits in thumb reach on every screen and opens a sheet over the form, so nothing is lost. On a computer it stays docked beside the form.

It opens with questions suited to the screen. Each answer cites its source document, adds a plain Heads up line where something could trip the person, and ends with the next questions as tappable cards. The card chosen stays marked, and the others fade.

When Guide can do something, such as text a link to finish on a phone, it says exactly what it will do and waits for a yes. If the person says stop, it confirms that nothing was sent or changed.

A thumbs up or down on each answer, a microphone for speaking a question, and a Learn tab that holds the whole library by topic.

At decision points a Worth asking card appears on its own: the amount, the bank connection, the rate, the approval and the loan agreement.

What it never does

Make or predict credit decisions. Choose an amount or a repayment option for someone. Give personal financial advice. Discourage anyone from applying.

Take an SSN or a bank login. If someone types one, it stops them and points to the secure field.

Answer from outside the approved documents. When it does not know, it says so and hands off to a person with the chat attached.

Hide that it is AI.

What it needs underneath

A versioned library of approved answers, each tied to a legal or program document, reviewed by compliance before release.

The chat saved with the application and treated as a consumer communication, with retention set. The model vendor reviewed as a service provider that handles borrower data.

A scripted test set run before every release, plus a fair lending review of the content and the logs.

Where it leads: the financial literacy platform

It is the same engine and the same library. Every question we write becomes a content asset. The questions people ask show where borrowers are confused, which tells us which guides to write and which screens to fix. After funding, it can meet people at the moments that matter, such as the first payment, the end of a grace period and a change of address.

On a computer

The computer version is not the phone stretched wide. People on a keyboard can take more on one page, see where they are, and read the full legal text without opening anything. It is the same flow, the same legal text and the same assistant, in a layout built for a larger screen.

Three columns

A steps rail on the left shows every stage, what is done, and the time left. The form sits in the middle at a readable width. Guide is docked on the right and is always there, not behind a button.

Below roughly 1180 pixels the assistant folds into an Ask button, and below 860 the rail becomes a thin progress bar. The phone version stays the primary design for small screens.

Fewer pages, same care

Eight pages to a rate. The start page holds the role, name, email, phone, the combined consent and the rates and fees block. Birthday, address and citizenship share one confirm page, and income and the cosigner question share another. Every consent still sits next to what it covers.

Legal text is shown inline. The loan disclosure opens beside its summary, with the full text in a scroll box. The credit and bank authorizations expand in place.

Made for a keyboard

Enter advances when a page is complete. Fields are labeled and tab in a sensible order, and every control shows a clear focus ring.

A code on the steps rail lets someone move to their phone in one scan. In the prototype it is a sample and does not scan. Documents can be added from the computer or sent from a phone.

Built for a thumb, tested for everyone

Inputs are 16 pixels or larger, so iPhones do not zoom in when a field is tapped.

Labels sit above fields and never disappear. A placeholder is an example, not a label.

Text meets 4.5 to 1 contrast. Errors and states use an icon and words, never color alone.

Screen readers get a labeled progress bar, a polite announcement while the rate loads, and labeled sheets.

Reduced motion is respected. Nothing animates for animation's sake.

Production build: first field focused on every screen, Enter advances, the button rides above the keyboard.

Plain words. Short sentences, second person, no program or underwriting detail in the copy.

Next: Spanish, dark mode, and a reading level check on every screen.

Gates before anything is built

This is a design prototype. Every legal paragraph comes from drafts marked not approved for borrower use. Counsel review comes first, and no counsel is engaged on this flow today.

1

Open merge fields show in [BRACKETS], such as the creditor, the servicer and the rate range. They are the drafts' own blanks, not placeholders we invented.

2

The 2.39% on the rate screen is a sample so the screen can be judged. It is not an offer. A rate shown in a borrower flow carries the full Reg Z and UDAAP treatment.

3

Every student signs the school self-certification form, which is decided. The drafted suite says its Reg Z forms apply only at Title IV schools, but the regulation text ties the rule to institutions of higher education as the Higher Education Act defines them. Counsel to confirm which of our schools qualify and which disclosure set applies if one does not. Open

4

The suite names who the creditor is as the first stop-ship item, and the Terms specimen names a bank partner as the lender. The screens use the LoanAmerica brand and leave the creditor fields bracketed. Open

5

The soft pull statement is only true if every Equifax request in the flow is a soft inquiry. The October 9 review rates it high exposure. It must be checked against the actual Equifax request configuration. High exposure

6

The tracker says the borrower has 3 business days to cancel after the Final Disclosure. LikelyThis follows Reg Z Subpart F, 12 CFR 1026.48. Confirm the wording and timing.

7

The H-18 and H-19 drafts carry NMLS and license lines. They are left out here, per the standing rule, and counsel decides what the forms must show. The only lender identity line is American Originations LLC (dba LoanAmerica).

8

Whether the Prism bank-data product is a consumer report is still open in the drafts, and it decides the adverse action wording and Consent B. The servicer's legal name is also unresolved.

9

A bank partner may set disclosure placement and consent wording. FinWise materials were not touched.

10

The assistant is AI. It must say so, stay inside explanation, and never decide, predict or steer. The planned posting statement that we do not use AI to review applications stays true only if that holds. Compliance to confirm

Decisions we still need

1

Is the rate check a soft check? Everything here assumes yes. Needs input from credit and risk, and from the bank partner.

2

School link only, or an open page too? The path depends on the school link. An open page adds school search and program questions.

3

School classification. Which schools are institutions of higher education under the Act? Add that as a field to school approval, so the school's page decides the disclosure set.

4

Cosigner credit check. Soft or hard? The cosigner screen leaves it open.

5

A rate before the hard check. The prototype shows one. Confirm with counsel and the bank partner.

6

Bank data provider. The drafts name Plaid and the vendor list has moved on. Name the provider so the consent can.

7

Housing. It is used to calculate debt to income on the back end, so it comes back in a simpler form: rent, own, or stay with friends or family for free, then the monthly payment only if they rent or own.

8

Passwordless sign-in. Check against the bank partner's security requirements.

9

Decline, expired-link and Final Disclosure screens. Next on the build list. The decline notices are drafted in the suite.

10

Affiliate sharing. The Privacy Notice assumes no sharing with affiliates. That is Carrie's call and it changes the notice.

11

Rate hold. The screens say nothing about how long a rate is held. Decide, then add one line.

12

Spanish. Worth planning now so no screen is built around English-only copy.

13

Assistant name, vendor and hosting. Who runs the model, where borrower data goes, and what the contract says about reuse of chats.

14

Chat retention. How long chats are kept, and whether they join the consent record.

15

Handoff and content ownership. Who answers when the assistant hands off, during what hours, and who approves the learning library.

16

What Guide may do, not just say. Texting a link is shown with an approve-first gate. Any further action needs the same gate and a review.

17

Name and look. Guide is a working name with a simple face. Pick the real name and character with the brand.

18

Prefill source. Which vendor can fill in the birthday and address from a phone number, and which fields does it actually return? The Is this you page depends on it.

19

Cosigner flow wording and counsel. The invitation text, the plain-words cosigner page and the 'nothing is final yet' line need review. Confirm the notice timing for cosigners.

20

Combined consent. Counsel to confirm that the terms, privacy, electronic paperwork, calls and texts, and school release can be one consent. The credit check, the bank connection and marketing stay separate.

21

Partners named on the phone consent. The list of who may call or text is saved in the product folder. Decide whether to name each partner or use a short line with the full list one tap away.

22

Rates and fees on the first page. Confirm the compressed block meets Reg Z form rules for electronic delivery, including a way to keep a copy, and that it opens only after the electronic consent.

23

School year and graduation date. SoFi's review liked them for timing consolidation outreach. Earnest's review said that level of detail is not needed. The prototype shows the program and expected finish as read-only, taken from the school.

24

State loan amount limits. Minimum and maximum amounts by state, and whether the cap is full tuition or tuition minus other aid. The prototype caps at tuition minus other aid. Needs research.

25

Social proof lines. Showing how many people pick an option needs our own real numbers with a source and date. None exist before launch, so the prototype shows none.

26

Dependents question. Parked as a possible back end question. Credit and counsel to review how it is asked and used under Regulation B.

27

How much to ask. Carrie's view is to prefill and confirm wherever possible. Emre's view is still to be recorded. The test for every question: can it be prefilled, read from the credit report or bank data, or inferred?

28

Decision speed. Earnest says five business days, which is far too long. Set a target we can keep, and only promise that.
